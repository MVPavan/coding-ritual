# Prose pass

Edit text a person will read so it says plainly what it means. The deliverable is
the edited text, with the same meaning. Read the draft once for what it claims, apply
the sections below, then compare the edit with the draft and restore any fact or
citation it lost. Leave code, commands and quotations as they are.

Three limits outrank every rule below:

- Change wording only, never facts, citations or code.
- Keep the tier words and hedges that mark uncertainty when editing a finished answer. They state how far a claim is known, so they are content and not filler.
- Never supply what the draft lacks. When a rule asks for a source, mechanism, figure or plan, use one only if the draft or its evidence holds it; otherwise take the rule's cut.

Source of the principles this pass applies to human prose: the Pruning section of `references/writing-principles.md` in `authoring-for-agents`.

## Claims and padding

- Delete a tacked-on present-participle phrase that claims significance, or back it with a real source.
- Name the source of any claim credited to unnamed groups, or delete the claim.
- Rewrite a sentence that conveys an impression so that it states a mechanism or number. If it cannot become an instruction, fact or figure, cut it.
- Shorten wordy stock phrases, and delete empty announcements of importance.
- Remove assistant pleasantries, offers of further help and excited discovery lines.
- Remove praise and agreement openers, and answer directly.
- Replace an optimistic sign-off with a specific plan or fact, or delete it when the draft holds neither.

## Words

- Replace these overused elevated words with plain ones: crucial, pivotal, vibrant, intricate, enduring, delve, foster, garner, showcase, underscore, enhance, tapestry, landscape, interplay, testament, additionally.
- Use is or has in place of a grand verb standing in for them.
- Use the common word over a fancier synonym.
- Keep one term for one referent instead of rotating through synonyms.
- Replace figurative pseudo-technical nouns with concrete words: substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, bedrock, modality, paradigm, gold-plating, endgame, north star, flywheel, and scaffolding or ratchet when not literal. Keep a word that names a real thing in this repository, as harness, surface and primitive do here.

## Sentences

- Replace the construction that denies one thing to promote another ("not just X, but Y") with a direct statement of the point.
- List as many items as naturally exist, never forcing a group of three.
- Replace a from-to construction that has no real scale with a direct list.
- Reduce piled-up qualifiers to a single modal, one that still carries the uncertainty.
- Split or trim any sentence the reader must reread to parse.
- Name the actor in place of a passive. Keep a passive only when the actor is unknown or does not matter.
- Cut an adverb, using a stronger verb or the measured figure in its place.
- Replace aphorisms, rhetorical fragments, personified code, figurative verbs and stock framing with literal phrasing.
- Restore articles, verbs and whole sentences, and spell out arrows, symbols and abbreviations.

## Punctuation and format

- Use no em dashes. End the sentence, or separate the thought with a comma or parentheses.
- Keep colons for lists and examples. Rewrite a mid-sentence colon and the comparison framing around it.
- Do not bold every proper noun or acronym.
- Turn a bullet whose bold label merely restates the line into prose. Keep a period-ended label followed by new detail.
- Write headings in sentence case.
- Remove ornamental emoji from headings and bullets.
- Replace typographic quotation marks with straight ones.
